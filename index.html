The Problem — Ireland's Retrofit Crisis

Ireland has a legally binding commitment: cut GHG emissions 51% by 2030. Current trajectory: ~29%. The gap lives almost entirely in residential heating — 500,000 homes need to be decarbonised by 2030. We've completed 55,000.

The conventional prescription — deep retrofit at €49,600 per home — is structurally impossible for roughly 30% of Irish housing stock. Pre-1940s solid-wall stone construction has no cavity to fill. Heat pumps require low-temperature radiators these homes don't have.

Only 12% of Irish households say they would try hydrogen heating, despite 53% wanting to change their heating system. The market exists — the demonstration is missing.

The Solution — Drop-In H₂ Technology

The H₂-ready boiler replaces only the gas burner — fitting existing radiators, pipework, thermostat, and zone controls without modification. Zero structural changes. Installed in one day. Cost: €3,000–6,000.

Fuel: green hydrogen produced from curtailed wind electricity at ~€0.01/kWh — the rate EirGrid pays to switch turbines off. Production cost: €1.5–2.5/kg versus €6–10/kg from grid electricity — a 4–6× cost advantage unique to Ireland's wind surplus. Only water vapour emitted.

Moneypoint — The 2029 Window

Moneypoint (Kilrush, Co. Clare) stopped burning coal in June 2025. ESB needs a domestic H₂ supplier confirmed before 2029 to proceed with turbine investment under Green Atlantic @ Moneypoint. Our curtailed wind revenues accumulate €21M/yr — €63M over 3 years self-funds a commercial electrolyser at Shannon. First delivery: 2028. We solve ESB's supplier problem. This is collaboration, not competition.

The 20% Covenant — Voluntary Farmer Transition

Governments that tried to mandate herd reduction failed politically. The 20% Covenant succeeds because it is voluntary, verifiable, and profitable. Participating farmers commit to 20% livestock reduction over 10 years in exchange for €12,000–18,000/yr H₂ transition subsidy plus carbon credits from Year 5. ICBF verifies via ear-tag data. No one loses income. No one is coerced.

The Thermodynamic Absurdity

Ireland sits in the Atlantic wind corridor. Yet Ireland imports ~70% of its energy and spends €8B+ annually on fossil fuels. Surplus wind is curtailed — while Ireland simultaneously imports expensive gas. 2.1 TWh wasted in 2025 = enough to power County Dublin for a year. EirGrid pays to waste it. Ireland pays again to import gas. Double the cost. Zero the benefit. That is thermodynamic absurdity.

This video shows a small-scale hydrogen demonstration system based on electrolysis. Electricity is applied to water, splitting it into hydrogen and oxygen gases. The generated hydrogen is then collected and released — validating the core principle at bench scale.

This is an early-stage prototype designed to validate the concept. The goal is to scale this system into a practical solution for storing excess renewable energy from wind power, converting it into usable hydrogen for home heating.

Investor Enquiry
Critical Investor Risk Analysis

Independent risk assessment — West of Ireland, 50 homes + 1 ha greenhouse

Top 5 Risks
1. Feedstock is not a resource — it's a scheduling problem
Curtailment is managed by EirGrid through constraint payments. You need signed offtake agreements with wind operators willing to divert output in real time. As Ireland adds interconnectors, curtailment drops — your cheapest input disappears.
2. Hydrogen storage near homes will not get planning permission easily
Compressed hydrogen above certain thresholds is a major hazard installation under COMAH regulations. Exclusion zones, third-party risk assessments required. No Irish local authority has approved a residential-adjacent hydrogen storage facility.
3. District heating CAPEX is the invisible killer
Delivering heat to 50 dispersed rural homes requires insulated piping, heat exchangers, controls, and metering. Ireland has almost no district heating infrastructure outside Dublin. Cost: €1.5M–€3M before buying a single electrolyzer.
4. Three revenue streams that conflict with each other
Heat supply needs continuous output. Grid balancing needs dispatchable on-demand response. Greenhouse needs sustained low-grade heat. A curtailment-fed electrolyzer cannot optimise for all three simultaneously.
5. 50 homes is a demonstration priced as a business
Electrolysis economics only work at multi-MW scale. At pilot scale, CAPEX per delivered kWh is 5–10× more expensive than a heat pump.
