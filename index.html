The Problem — Ireland's Retrofit Crisis

Ireland has a legally binding commitment: cut GHG emissions 51% by 2030. Current trajectory: ~29%. The gap lives almost entirely in residential heating — 500,000 homes need to be decarbonised by 2030. We've completed 55,000.

The conventional prescription — deep retrofit at €49,600 per home — is structurally impossible for roughly 30% of Irish housing stock. Pre-1940s solid-wall stone construction has no cavity to fill. Heat pumps require low-temperature radiators these homes don't have.

Only 12% of Irish households say they would try hydrogen heating, despite 53% wanting to change their heating system. The market exists — the demonstration is missing.

The Solution — Drop-In H₂ Technology

The H₂-ready boiler replaces only the gas burner — fitting existing radiators, pipework, thermostat, and zone controls without modification. Zero structural changes. Installed in one day. Cost: €3,000–6,000.

Fuel: green hydrogen produced from curtailed wind electricity at ~€0.01/kWh. Production cost: €1.5–2.5/kg versus €6–10/kg from grid electricity — a 4–6× cost advantage unique to Ireland's wind surplus. Only water vapour emitted.

This video shows a small-scale hydrogen demonstration system based on electrolysis. Electricity is applied to water, splitting it into hydrogen and oxygen gases. The generated hydrogen is then collected and released — validating the core principle at bench scale.

This is an early-stage prototype designed to validate the concept. The goal is to scale this system into a practical solution for storing excess renewable energy from wind power, converting it into usable hydrogen for home heating.

Investor Enquiry
Critical Investor Risk Analysis

Independent risk assessment — West of Ireland, 50 homes + 1 ha greenhouse

Top 5 Risks
1. Feedstock is not a resource — it's a scheduling problem
Curtailment is managed by EirGrid through constraint payments. You need signed offtake agreements with wind operators. As Ireland adds interconnectors, curtailment drops — your cheapest input disappears.
2. Hydrogen storage near homes will not get planning permission easily
Compressed hydrogen above certain thresholds is a major hazard under COMAH regulations. No Irish local authority has approved a residential-adjacent hydrogen storage facility.
3. District heating CAPEX is the invisible killer
Delivering heat to 50 dispersed rural homes requires insulated piping, heat exchangers, controls, and metering. Cost: €1.5M–€3M before buying a single electrolyzer.
4. Three revenue streams that conflict with each other
Heat supply needs continuous output. Grid balancing needs dispatchable response. Greenhouse needs sustained low-grade heat. A curtailment-fed electrolyzer cannot optimise for all three simultaneously.
5. 50 homes is a demonstration priced as a business
Electrolysis economics only work at multi-MW scale. At pilot scale, CAPEX per delivered kWh is 5–10× more expensive than a heat pump.
Bottom line: Technically interesting concept on unvalidated assumptions. Spend €50K–€100K on feasibility first — real vendor quotes, site studies, planning pre-application — before asking for serious capital.
